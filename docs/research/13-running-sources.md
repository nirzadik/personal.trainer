Research date: 2026-11-08. Supersedes the unverified parts of `06-running.md`, which was written mostly from search snippets.

**Method and access.** Full texts and abstracts were read from PMC (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) and Frontiers (www.frontiersin.org). ## What changed in running.md and why

1. **Single-run rule promoted to rule #1, applied to every run, not only the long run** (Frandsen [V]). Added the authors' caveat: at most one "new longest" step per week, because chained +10% runs are still excessive. **Dropped the "+1 km floor below 8 km"**: at 5 km, +1 km is a 20% spike, which is exactly the >10-30% band with HRR 1.64. Short runs now round down (5 → 5.5 km, 30 → 33 min). *Note for the app QA step:* `plan.js` LONG_RUN_SPIKE (×1.3 and >+3 km) is looser than the evidence. It may stay as a hard-error threshold, but a ×1.1 warning would match the skill.
2. **Weekly 10% rule** is kept as a ceiling [P]. The evidence wording now reflects Fredette 2022 (conflicting evidence) and Ramskov 2022 (no pace × volume interaction).
3. **Riegel/Vickers corrected.** running.md said Vickers' "fitted exponent was nearer 1.15". That figure came from a blog (Downey), not the paper. Vickers used k = 1.07, and Riegel's own recreational value was 1.05-1.06. The table and the +5% marathon adjustment [P] are kept.
4. **Intensity distribution.** The 2024 meta-analysis figures are now verified (Oliveira). The secondary-summary claim of an "81/3/15 best distribution" is removed as unverified. Festa 2020 is added (threshold-focused training is equally effective and takes less time), which supports a pyramidal default for time-limited hybrid athletes.
5. **Strength → injury claim corrected.** The old text said strength training "lowers injury risk" in runners, citing Lauersen 2014, which pooled all sports and had only 4 strength studies. In runners specifically: Wu 2024 found no significant overall effect (supervised programmes only), and Toresdahl 2019 was null. running.md now sells strength for economy and performance (Blagrove; Llanos-Lagos), not as injury insurance.
6. **"Running interferes more than cycling" (Wilson 2012) removed.** The newer and larger Schumann 2022 found no modality difference. Same-session separation is now **≥3 h** (Schumann) to **6 h** [P]. The trained-lifter caveat is added from Petré 2021.
7. **Taper.** Bosquet's figures are confirmed via Wang 2023. Added Smyth 2021 (strict 3-week marathon taper; observational). The marathon taper is now a 3-week progression.
8. **Speed vs volume.** Added Warden 2021: bone load rises faster with speed, so speed is dropped first and returned last, and volume is held when intensity is added.
9. **New content.** Weekly templates for 1, 2, 3 and 4+ runs/week by goal (general/hybrid, 5K-10K, half, marathon) [P, built from the rules above]. A worked long-run progression with cutbacks. A no-race pace-setting protocol: talk test/RPE, then a 3K/mile/5K trial with Riegel conversions (3K × 1.72, mile × 3.33 = (5/3)^1.06 and (5/1.609)^1.06). Hybrid rules: ≥2 HIIT/week means 0-1 quality runs; machine-based HIIT spares the legs. A VO2max rep progression.
10. **Cut or merged:** the separate Z1/Z2 rows (now one Easy row with a talk-test anchor), the VDOT column (not needed for lookup), the duplicated principles, and the redundant scheduling detail that lives in `conditioning.md` §10. Final size 19.9 KB (from 21.1 KB).

## Remaining gaps

- Daniels' long-run share (≤25-30%) and per-session T/I/R caps, and Pfitzinger/Daniels race-build ranges, are still unverified against the books [P/K].
- NHS Couch to 5K week-by-week (nhs.uk not allowed) [K].
- Return-after-break tiers are practitioner heuristics [P]; no primary detraining source was read.
- Strides and hill sprints have no direct trial evidence located [P].
- Daniels' 4th-edition Easy range may be slower than the 62-70% VDOT used here. Because Easy is treated as a ceiling, a slower easy pace is always acceptable, which limits the risk.
