## 1. NASM-CPT

### 1.1 OPT Model (Optimum Performance Training)
Three levels: **Stabilization -> Strength -> Power**, split into five phases. Current (6th/7th ed.) numbering puts Maximal Strength at Phase 4 and Power at Phase 5. Some older materials number them differently. [V]
- Phase 1 Stabilization Endurance (Stabilization level)
- Phase 2 Strength Endurance (Strength level)
- Phase 3 Muscular Development / Hypertrophy (Strength level)
- Phase 4 Maximal Strength (Strength level)
- Phase 5 Power (Power level)

Every client starts in Phase 1. General-fitness and weight-loss clients mostly cycle Phases 1-2(-3). Clients do not have to move through every phase in order after their initial stabilization block. Typical phase length is about 4 weeks (Phase 1 is 4-6 weeks; NASM blog: "two to six weeks" in Phase 4). [V/T]

**Resistance-training acute variables (NASM 7th ed.)**

| Variable | Ph 1 Stab. Endurance | Ph 2 Strength Endurance | Ph 3 Hypertrophy | Ph 4 Max Strength | Ph 5 Power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reps | 12-20 | 8-12 | 6-12 | 1-5 | 1-5 (strength) + 8-10 (power) |
| Sets | 1-3 | 2-4 | 3-5 [!] (NASM blog 3-6) | 4-6 [!] (some 3-5) | 3-5 [!] (some 3-6) |
| Tempo (ecc/iso/con) | 4/2/1 (slow) | Strength exercise 2/0/2; stab. exercise 4/2/1 | 2/0/2 | X/X/X (as fast as can be controlled) | Strength X/X/X; power as fast as possible |
| Intensity | 50-70% 1RM | 70-80% 1RM | 75-85% 1RM | 85-100% 1RM | Strength 85-100% 1RM; power ~30-45% 1RM or <=10% BW (med ball) |
| Rest | 0-90 s | 0-60 s (between pair); 1-2 min between superset rounds | 0-60 s | 3-5 min | 1-2 min between pair; 3-5 min between circuits |
| Frequency | 2-4x/wk | 2-4x/wk | 3-6x/wk | 2-4x/wk | 2-4x/wk |
| Duration | 4-6 wk | 4 wk | 4 wk | 4 wk | 4 wk |
| Exercise selection | 1-2 stabilization-progression exercises per body part (proprioceptively enriched, e.g. single-leg, stability ball) | Superset: 1 strength + 1 stabilization exercise with similar joint actions (e.g. bench press -> stability-ball push-up) | 2-4 strength exercises per body part | 1-3 strength exercises per body part | Superset (complex/contrast): 1 strength + 1 power exercise (e.g. bench press -> med-ball chest pass) |

Sources: NASM blog phase pages ([Phase 1](https://blog.nasm.org/opt-model-phase-1), [Phase 3](https://blog.nasm.org/phase-3-muscular-development-training), [Phase 4](https://blog.nasm.org/phase-4-maximal-strength-training), [Phase 5](https://blog.nasm.org/phase-5-power-training)); [NASM trainer: OPT strength](https://trainer.nasm.org/cpt-basics-opt-model-strength); [Ironmaster summary](https://ironmaster.com/blog/fundamentals-of-resistance-training-part-1/); [open-exam-prep NASM 7.3](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/nasm-cpt/07-opt-model-and-program-design-framework/strength-endurance-hypertrophy-and-maximal-strength).

Evidence note: a Brookbush Institute review found power output was best after 4-8 min rest following a heavy set, compared with NASM's 1-2 min ([Brookbush](https://brookbushinstitute.com/articles/power-workout-strength-power-supersets)). The skill should treat NASM rest periods as a framework, not a physiological optimum.

**Other OPT components by level [T].** These matter for whole-session templates.
- Workout template order: Warm-up (SMR + static stretch in stabilization; SMR + active-isolated in strength; SMR + dynamic in power) -> Core / Balance / Plyometric (Reactive) -> SAQ (optional) -> Resistance -> Client-chosen -> Cool-down.
- Core / balance / plyo by level: stabilization uses little spinal/pelvic motion, 1-4 exercises, 12-20 reps, slow 4/2/1 (plyo: hold landing 3-5 s). Strength uses dynamic eccentric/concentric moves through full ROM, 8-12 reps, medium tempo. Power uses fast movements, 8-12 reps (plyo 8-10), x/x/x.
- Flexibility continuum: corrective (SMR + static) -> active (SMR + active-isolated, 5-10 reps holding 1-2 s) -> functional (SMR + dynamic, 3-10 exercises, 10 reps).

### 1.2 Overhead Squat Assessment (OHSA)
**Procedure [V/T].** Shoes off. Feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointing straight ahead, foot and ankle in neutral. Arms overhead with elbows fully extended and upper arms in line with the torso. Squat to roughly chair height, then return. Do 5 reps from the anterior view and 5 from the lateral view (posterior view optional). The OHSA is a movement screen, **not a diagnostic tool** (NASM states this). Modified versions: heels elevated (if forward lean or low-back change normalizes, suspect limited ankle dorsiflexion: tight calves), and hands on hips (if low back arch or forward lean improves, suspect lats/pecs).

Kinetic-chain checkpoints: foot/ankle, knee, lumbo-pelvic-hip complex (LPHC), shoulders/thoracic, head/cervical.

**Compensation table (NASM CPT 7th ed. / CES Overhead Squat Solutions Table) [T, partially V]**

| View | Compensation | Probable OVERACTIVE (inhibit + lengthen) | Probable UNDERACTIVE (activate + integrate) | Example correctives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anterior | **Feet turn out** | Soleus, lateral gastrocnemius, biceps femoris (short head) | Medial gastrocnemius, medial hamstring complex (semitendinosus/semimembranosus), gracilis, sartorius, popliteus | SMR/stretch calves and biceps femoris; single-leg balance reach; activate medial hamstrings |
| Anterior | **Feet flatten** (CPT 7th ed.) | Peroneals, lateral gastrocnemius, biceps femoris, TFL | Anterior and posterior tibialis, medial gastrocnemius, gluteus medius | Calf/peroneal SMR; short-foot; tib anterior activation |

| Anterior | **Knees move outward (varus)** (CES) | Piriformis, biceps femoris, TFL, gluteus minimus/medius | Adductor complex, medial hamstrings, gluteus maximus | Piriformis/TFL SMR + stretch; adductor activation |
| Lateral | **Excessive forward lean** | Soleus, gastrocnemius, hip flexor complex, abdominal complex (rectus abdominis, external obliques) | Anterior tibialis, gluteus maximus, erector spinae | SMR calves and quads; stretch calves and hip flexors; floor cobra; ball squat |

| Lateral | **Head juts forward** (7th ed.) | Upper trapezius, sternocleidomastoid, levator scapulae | Deep cervical flexors | Chin tucks |

Opposite side: TFL, piriformis, biceps femoris, gluteus medius | Same side as shift: gluteus medius, anterior tibialis. Opposite side: adductor complex | Unilateral SMR + stretch; side-lying abduction; single-leg work |

Linked dysfunction patterns: **Lower crossed syndrome** (low back arches + forward lean: tight hip flexors and erectors, weak glutes and abdominals). **Upper crossed syndrome** (arms fall forward / head forward: tight pecs, upper traps, levator; weak deep cervical flexors, mid/lower traps, rhomboids). **Pronation distortion syndrome** (feet flatten/turn out + knees in).

Other NASM assessments worth knowing: single-leg squat (knee valgus), push / pull assessments (shoulders elevate, head forward, low back arches), Davies test, shark skill test, upper-extremity / lower-extremity strength tests (power level only), YMCA 3-min step test, Rockport walk, 1.5-mile run.

### 1.3 Corrective Exercise Continuum (NASM CES)
Done in this order, each step preparing the next [V]:

| Phase | Purpose | Modality | Acute variables (NASM CPT 7th / CES 2nd ed.) [T] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Inhibit** | Reduce overactivity / neural drive of overactive tissue | Self-myofascial release (foam roller, ball) | Hold tender spots ~30 s (range 30-90 s in CES 2nd ed.); 1 set per area; slow rolling |
| **2. Lengthen** | Restore extensibility of overactive tissue | Static stretching; neuromuscular stretching (NMS / PNF) | Static: hold 30 s, 1-3 sets (NMS: 7-10 s contraction at ~25% then stretch) |
| **3. Activate** | Re-educate / strengthen underactive muscles | Isolated strengthening; positional isometrics | Isolated: 1-2 sets, 10-15 reps, 4/2/1 tempo (2 s isometric hold at end range). Positional isometrics: 4 reps at 25/50/75/100% effort, each held about 4 s |
| **4. Integrate** | Retrain the synergistic movement pattern | Integrated dynamic movements (e.g. ball squat with overhead press, step-up to balance) | 1-3 sets, 10-15 reps, controlled tempo; progress complexity, keeping posture |

Rule of thumb: **lengthen what is overactive and activate what is underactive**. Correctives sit in the warm-up and can be the whole session for deconditioned clients.
Sources: [NASM guide to the CEx continuum](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/recovery/a-guide-to-nasms-corrective-exercise-continuum); [NASM CES 4 keys](https://blog.nasm.org/ces/nasms-corrective-exercise-continuum-4-keys-fixing-movement-dysfunction/).

## 2. ACSM Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription (GETP)

**Edition status [V]:** The **12th edition** (Lippincott/Wolters Kluwer; dated 2024/2025, ISBN 9781975219208) is current. ACSM certification exams switched to GETP12 on **10 July 2025**. Main changes: revised FITT tables, a significant update to the resistance-training section (Ch. 5), updated behavioral theories (Ch. 12), and new sections on sex differences, transgender and gender-diverse individuals, SCAD, MASLD, ME/CFS, and respiratory muscle training. Sources: [ACSM GETP12 page](https://www.acsm.org/education-resources/books/guidelines-exercise-testing-prescription); [ACE summary of GETP updates](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/pros/expert-articles/8996/updates-to-acsm-s-guidelines-for-exercise-testing-and-prescription/); [ACSM exam GETP12 effective date](https://acsm.org/tag/acsm-guidelines); [ACSM "What's New" course](https://www.pathlms.com/acsm/courses/107768).

**New 2026 ACSM Resistance Training Position Stand [V]:** "Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews", *MSSE* 2026;58(4):851-872. It replaces the 2009 progression-models stand and draws on 137 reviews. Key messages:
- Train all major muscle groups **>=2 days/wk** and build up gradually.
- **Participation and adherence matter more than exact prescription variables.**
- Strength: heavier loads (**>=80% 1RM**), roughly 2-3 sets per exercise.
- Hypertrophy is driven mainly by volume, with **a minimum of about 10 sets per muscle group per week** and a dose-response beyond that. A wide load range works when sets are hard.
- Power / function: moderate loads (**30-70% 1RM**) moved at high velocity, especially useful for older adults.
- Training to failure, equipment type, set structure, time under tension, BFR and periodization model did **not consistently** change outcomes.

Sources: [ACSM science spotlight](https://acsm.org/science-spotlight-acsm-releases-new-position-stand-on-resistance-training/); [ACSM infographic](https://acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Position-Stand-infographic.pdf); [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/acsm-unveils-landmark-2026-resistance-training-guidelines-first-update-in-17-years); [NFPT analysis](https://nfpt.com/resistance-training-as-the-engine-of-performance-what-the-2026-acsm-position-stand-means-for-strength-energy-and-real-world-function/); [Physio-pedia](https://members.physio-pedia.com/members-news/new-acsm-resistance-training-guidelines-what-physiotherapists-need-to-know/). The 10-set and 80% figures come from secondary summaries.

### 2.1 Intensity classification (ACSM) [T]

| Intensity | %HRR or %VO2R | %HRmax | METs | RPE (6-20) | Talk test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Very light | <30 | <57 | <2.0 | <9 | |
| Light | 30-39 | 57-63 | 2.0-2.9 | 9-11 | Can sing |
| Moderate | 40-59 | 64-76 | 3.0-5.9 | 12-13 | Can talk, not sing |
| Vigorous | 60-89 | 77-95 | 6.0-8.7 | 14-17 | Only a few words |
| Near-max / max | >=90 | >=96 | >=8.8 | >=18 | |

- Karvonen formula: Target HR = ((HRmax - HRrest) x %intensity) + HRrest.
- HRmax estimates: 220 - age; Tanaka 208 - 0.7 x age; Gellish 207 - 0.7 x age.
- On the 0-10 OMNI/CR10 scale: moderate = 5-6, vigorous = 7-8.

### 2.2 FITT-VP for healthy adults (GETP 11/12) [T]

**Cardiorespiratory (aerobic)**
- **F:** >=5 d/wk moderate, or >=3 d/wk vigorous, or 3-5 d/wk of the two combined.
- **I:** moderate (40-59% HRR/VO2R) and/or vigorous (60-89%). Light-to-moderate suits deconditioned people.
- **T:** 30-60 min/d moderate (>=150 min/wk), 20-60 min/d vigorous (>=75 min/wk), or a combination. Bouts can be accumulated; the 2018 PAG dropped the >=10-min bout minimum.
- **T (type):** rhythmic activity using large muscle groups that needs little skill. HIIT is acceptable.
- **V:** target >=500-1,000 MET-min/wk. Pedometer guide: >=7,000 steps/d (raise by 1,000/d per week as tolerated).
- **P:** gradual. Start by raising duration 5-10 min per session every 1-2 weeks over the first 4-6 weeks, then adjust over 4-8 months.

**Resistance**
- **F:** 2-3 d/wk per major muscle group, with >=48 h between sessions for the same group.
- **I:** novice-intermediate 60-70% 1RM (strength); experienced >=80%; older or very deconditioned beginners 40-50%; muscular endurance <50%; power in older adults 20-50% moved fast.
- **T (sets/reps):** 8-12 reps for strength and power; 10-15 for older/deconditioned beginners; 15-25 for endurance. 2-4 sets (a single set works for beginners and older adults). Rest 2-3 min between sets.
- **T (type):** 8-10 multi-joint exercises covering all major muscle groups, with agonist/antagonist balance. Single-joint and core exercises are optional.
- **V/P:** progress gradually through load, reps or frequency. One common rule is +2-10% load when the client can do 1-2 reps beyond target.

**Flexibility**
- **F:** >=2-3 d/wk; daily is most effective.
- **I:** stretch to the point of feeling tightness or slight discomfort.
- **T:** static holds 10-30 s for most adults; 30-60 s for older adults. PNF: 3-6 s contraction at 20-75% MVC, then a 10-30 s assisted stretch.
- **T (type):** static (active or passive), dynamic, ballistic, PNF.
- **V:** about 60 s total per joint (e.g. 2-4 reps), and it works best after a warm-up.

**Neuromotor (balance, agility, coordination, gait, tai chi, yoga)**
- **F:** >=2-3 d/wk. **T:** >=20-30 min/d (about 60 min/wk). **I:** not established. Recommended especially for older adults and frequent fallers.

### 2.3 Physical activity recommendations (2018 Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans / WHO 2020; ACSM-endorsed) [T]
- **Adults:** 150-300 min/wk moderate, or 75-150 min/wk vigorous, or an equivalent combination. Muscle strengthening (all major groups) on >=2 d/wk. More than 300 min/wk adds benefit. Move more and sit less, since any activity counts.
- **Older adults (65+):** same as adults, plus **multicomponent** activity (balance + aerobic + strength). Do as much as conditions allow.
- **Children 6-17:** >=60 min/d moderate-to-vigorous, including vigorous, muscle-strengthening and bone-strengthening each >=3 d/wk.
- **Pregnancy / postpartum:** >=150 min/wk moderate.
- **Chronic conditions / disability:** follow the adult guidelines as able.

### 2.4 Pre-participation health screening (Riebe et al., MSSE 2015; carried into GETP 10-12) [V]
The algorithm rests on three factors: (1) **current physical activity** (planned, structured exercise of >=30 min at moderate intensity on >=3 d/wk for at least the last 3 months), (2) **known cardiovascular, metabolic (type 1 or type 2 diabetes) or renal disease** and/or **signs or symptoms**, and (3) the **desired exercise intensity**. **Risk-factor counting was dropped from the decision.**

| Currently exercising? | Disease / symptoms | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| No | No CV/metabolic/renal disease, no symptoms | Medical clearance not necessary. Start light-moderate and progress gradually per ACSM. |
| No | Known disease, asymptomatic | Medical clearance recommended. After clearance, start light-moderate and progress. |
| No | Any signs or symptoms (with or without disease) | Medical clearance recommended before any exercise. |
| Yes | No disease, no symptoms | No clearance needed. Continue moderate or vigorous and progress. |
| Yes | Known disease, asymptomatic | No clearance needed for moderate. Clearance recommended before **vigorous**. |
| Yes | Any signs or symptoms | **Stop exercising** and seek medical clearance before resuming. |

**Signs and symptoms suggestive of CV/metabolic/renal disease (major red flags):**
1. Pain or discomfort (or other anginal equivalent) in the chest, neck, jaw, arms or other areas that may come from ischemia
2. Shortness of breath at rest or with mild exertion
3. Dizziness or syncope
4. Orthopnea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea
5. Ankle edema
6. Palpitations or tachycardia
7. Intermittent claudication
8. Known heart murmur
9. Unusual fatigue or shortness of breath with usual activities

Sources: [Riebe et al. 2015 (URI repository)](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/kinesiology_facpubs/180); [ACSM blog on new screening](https://www.acsm.org/blog-detail/acsm-certified-blog/2018/02/01/exercise-preparticipation-screening-removing-barriers-initiating-exercise); [NHANES validation, PMC7059860](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7059860). NHANES data on adults 40+ showed 54.2% would be referred before starting any exercise, and 2.6% only before vigorous exercise.

**Absolute stop / termination signs during exercise (ACSM, nonclinical setting) [T]:** onset of angina-like symptoms; a fall in SBP of >=10 mmHg as workload rises; excessive BP rise (SBP >250 or DBP >115 mmHg); shortness of breath, wheezing, leg cramps or claudication; signs of poor perfusion (light-headedness, confusion, ataxia, pallor, cyanosis, nausea, cold clammy skin); HR that fails to rise with intensity; a noticeable change in heart rhythm; the client asks to stop; severe fatigue; equipment failure.

### 2.5 Special populations highlights [T unless marked]

**Hypertension**
- BP categories (ACC/AHA 2017, used by ACSM): normal <120/<80; elevated 120-129/<80; stage 1 130-139 or 80-89; stage 2 >=140 or >=90.
- Aerobic: 5-7 d/wk, moderate (40-59% HRR, RPE 12-13), 30-60 min/d continuous or accumulated.
- Resistance: 2-3 d/wk, 60-70% 1RM (up to 80%), 2-4 sets of 8-12. Older or novice clients start at 40-50%.
- Isometric handgrip training is an emerging option.
- Flexibility: >=2-3 d/wk.
- Expected effect: a **5-8 mmHg** reduction in hypertensive adults [V]. Exercise also produces acute post-exercise hypotension.
- Cautions:
  - Resting **SBP >=200 or DBP >=110 mmHg means do not exercise**; refer [V, via study guide].
  - Avoid the Valsalva maneuver and long breath-holding / heavy isometrics.
  - Beta-blockers blunt HR, so use RPE and talk test instead.
  - Alpha-blockers, calcium-channel blockers and vasodilators raise post-exercise hypotension risk, so extend the cool-down.
  - Diuretics raise dehydration and heat risk.
- Sources: [Pescatello 2015 PMC4589552](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4589552/); [ACSM 2004 position stand, PubMed 15076798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15076798/); [ACSM exercise & hypertension](https://acsm.org/exercise-hypertension).

**Type 2 diabetes (ACSM consensus, Kanaley et al., MSSE 2022;54(2):353-368) [V]**
- Aerobic: 3-7 d/wk with no more than 2 consecutive days off. Moderate-vigorous intensity, >=150 min/wk. Post-meal activity lowers glucose; bouts of >=45 min give the most consistent benefit. Breaking up sitting every 30 min with light activity helps.
- Resistance: >=2-3 non-consecutive d/wk, moderate (50-69% 1RM) to vigorous (70-85%). 1-3 sets of 10-15 reps, progressing to 8-10 reps at heavier loads.
- Flexibility and balance: 2-3 d/wk, especially for older adults and clients with neuropathy.
- Glucose safety:
  - Do not start exercise if BG is >250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) **with** moderate or high ketones.
  - Use caution above 300 mg/dL (16.7 mmol/L) without ketones: start only if feeling well and stay hydrated.
  - Clients on insulin or secretagogues (sulfonylureas) should take carbohydrate (about 15-30 g if BG <90-100 mg/dL before exercise) or have their **clinician** adjust insulin. A trainer never adjusts medication.
  - Hypoglycemia signs: shakiness, sweating, confusion, hunger, anxiety, headache. Treat with **15 g fast carbohydrate and recheck after 15 min (the 15-15 rule)**.
- Complications:
  - Proliferative retinopathy: avoid vigorous effort, heavy lifting, Valsalva and head-down positions.
  - Peripheral neuropathy: proper footwear and daily foot checks.
  - Autonomic neuropathy: blunted HR response (use RPE), abnormal BP, poor thermoregulation, higher hypoglycemia risk.
- Sources: [Guideline Central summary](https://guidelinecentral.com/guideline/1337044); [Consultant360](https://www.consultant360.com/exclusive/acsm-updates-physical-activity-guidelines-type-2-diabetes); [medicaldialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/diabetes-endocrinology/guidelines/acsm-consensus-statement-on-physical-activity-in-patients-with-type-2-diabetes-87507).

**Overweight / obesity**
- Aerobic: >=5 d/wk, starting at moderate intensity (40-59% HRR) and progressing to >=60%.
  - Start at 30 min/d (150 min/wk) and progress to 60 min/d, i.e. **250-300 min/wk (>=2,000 kcal/wk)**, for long-term weight loss and preventing regain.
- Resistance: 2-3 d/wk, 60-70% 1RM, 8-12 reps, 2-4 sets, to preserve lean mass.
- Clinical target: **5-10% weight loss over 3-6 months**. Energy deficit and diet are handled with an RD or physician; a trainer gives general guidance only.
- Practical points: low-impact modes; check equipment weight limits; heat intolerance; joint stress.

**Older adults (>=65, or 50-64 with clinically significant conditions)**
- Aerobic: moderate (5-6 on a 0-10 scale) 30-60 min/d for >=150 min/wk, or vigorous (7-8/10) 20-30 min/d for >=75 min/wk.
- Resistance: >=2 d/wk; start at 40-50% 1RM (very light), progressing to 60-80%. 8-10 exercises, 1-3 sets of 8-12 (start with 10-15). Include **power training** (fast concentric at 30-60% 1RM).
- Flexibility: >=2 d/wk, 30-60 s holds.
- Neuromotor / balance: >=2-3 d/wk, especially for frequent fallers or those with mobility limits.
- Watch for orthostatic hypotension, medication effects (beta-blockers) and osteoporosis (avoid loaded spinal flexion and twisting).

**Pregnancy (ACOG Committee Opinion 804, 2020; reaffirmed) [V/T]**
- Healthy pregnant people should get **>=150 min/wk moderate** activity, spread over >=3 days (Canadian 2019 guideline). Intensity: RPE 13-14 or the talk test. People already doing vigorous exercise can usually continue with clinician approval.
- Avoid:
  - lying supine for long periods after about 20 weeks
  - contact sports and activities with a high fall risk
  - scuba diving
  - hot yoga / hyperthermia
  - prolonged Valsalva
  - exertion above about 6,000 ft unless acclimatized
- **Absolute contraindications:**
  - hemodynamically significant heart disease
  - restrictive lung disease
  - incompetent cervix or cerclage
  - multiple gestation at risk of premature labor
  - persistent 2nd/3rd-trimester bleeding
  - placenta previa after 26 weeks
  - premature labor
  - ruptured membranes
  - preeclampsia or pregnancy-induced hypertension
  - severe anemia
- **Relative contraindications:**
  - poorly controlled type 1 diabetes, hypertension, seizure disorder or hyperthyroidism
  - chronic bronchitis
  - heavy smoking
  - extreme underweight
  - orthopedic limitations
  - fetal growth restriction
  - mild/moderate anemia
- **Stop and refer if any of these occur:**
  - vaginal bleeding
  - regular painful contractions
  - amniotic fluid leakage
  - dyspnea before exertion
  - dizziness
  - headache
  - chest pain
  - muscle weakness affecting balance
  - calf pain or swelling
- A trainer needs the client's **obstetric provider clearance** (e.g. Get Active Questionnaire for Pregnancy / PARmed-X for Pregnancy).
- Sources: [ACOG CO 804 (QxMD)](https://read.qxmd.com/read/32217980/physical-activity-and-exercise-during-pregnancy-and-the-postpartum-period-acog-committee-opinion-number-804); [MDedge summary](https://mdedge.com/content/new-acog-recommendations-encourage-exercise-during-pregnancy); [DONA summary](https://dona.org/new-acog-opinion-on-exercise-in-the-childbearing-year/).

## 3. NSCA (CSCS / NSCA-CPT) — Essentials of Strength Training & Conditioning, 4th ed.

### 3.1 Needs analysis [T]
1. **Evaluate the sport / activity:**
   - Movement analysis: limb and body movement patterns and the muscles involved.
   - Physiological analysis: strength, power, hypertrophy and muscular endurance priorities; energy systems.
   - Injury analysis: common joint and muscle injury sites and their causes.
2. **Assess the athlete / client:**
   - Training status: current program, training background and experience (technique), and the intensity and volume of recent training.
   - Physical testing and evaluation.
   - Primary resistance-training goal.
   - For a CPT, also: health and injury history and client preferences / time available.

### 3.2 Exercise selection and order [T]
- **Core exercises:** multi-joint, recruit large muscle areas, direct sport application. **Structural** core exercises load the spine directly (e.g. back squat), and power/explosive structural lifts belong here.
- **Assistance exercises:** single-joint, small muscle areas, injury prevention / rehab.
- **Order options:**
  1. **Power -> other core -> assistance** (e.g. power clean -> squat -> leg curl). Most fatiguing and technical lifts go first.
  2. **Alternate upper and lower body** (lets muscles recover; circuit-friendly; good for untrained clients).
  3. **Alternate push and pull.**
  4. **Supersets** (two exercises for opposing muscle groups) and **compound sets** (two exercises for the same group).
- **Training frequency by status:**
  - Beginner: 2-3 sessions/wk.
  - Intermediate: 3 sessions/wk, or 4 with a split routine.
  - Advanced: 4-7 sessions/wk.
  - Allow at least 1 rest day but no more than 3 between sessions for the same muscle group.

### 3.3 %1RM vs. repetitions (NSCA training load chart, Landers 1984) [V]

| %1RM | Reps possible |
|---|---|
| 100 | 1 |
| 95 | 2 |
| 93 | 3 |
| 90 | 4 |
| 87 | 5 |
| 85 | 6 |
| 83 | 7 |
| 80 | 8 |
| 77 | 9 |
| 75 | 10 |
| 70 | 11 |
| 67 | 12 |
| 65 | 15 |

Caveats: the relationship varies with exercise (machines and lower-body lifts allow more reps at the same %), with training status and with sex. A 2024 meta-regression of about 7,000 people (Nuzzo et al.) suggests the NSCA table slightly underestimates reps at a given %. Sources: [NSCA training load chart PDF](https://dxpprod.nsca.com/contentassets/61d813865e264c6e852cadfe247eae52/nsca_training_load_chart.pdf); [Nuzzo 2024, PMC10933212](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933212); [Nuzzo Letter](https://jameslnuzzo.substack.com/p/the-repetitions-1rm-relationship).

### 3.4 Training goal assignments (NSCA Tables 17.8 / 17.12 / 17.13) [T, consistent with V snippets]

| Goal | Load (%1RM) | Goal reps | Sets* | Rest between sets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | >=85 | <=6 | 2-6 | 2-5 min |
| **Power, single-effort** (e.g. shot put, jump) | 80-90 | 1-2 | 3-5 | 2-5 min |
| **Power, multiple-effort** (e.g. basketball) | 75-85 | 3-5 | 3-5 | 2-5 min |
| **Hypertrophy** | 67-85 | 6-12 | 3-6 | 30-90 s |
| **Muscular endurance** | <=67 | >=12 | 2-3 | <=30 s |

*Sets apply to core exercises; assistance exercises usually get 1-3 sets.
- Power loads are lower than the strength table implies so that bar velocity stays high. Plyometrics and Olympic-lift derivatives belong here.
- **"2-for-2 rule"** for load progression: if the client does 2 or more reps beyond the goal on the last set in 2 consecutive sessions, increase load next session.
  - Upper body, less-trained / smaller clients: +2.5-5 lb (1-2 kg). Upper body, more-trained / larger clients: +5-10+ lb (2-4+ kg).
  - Lower body, less-trained / smaller clients: +10-15 lb (4-7 kg). Lower body, more-trained / larger clients: +15-20+ lb (7-9+ kg).
  - Alternatively, about +2.5-10%.
- Sources: [The Movement System CSCS program design](https://www.themovementsystem.com/blog/cscs-program-design); [Achievable NSCA load chart](https://achievable.me/define/nsca-load-chart/).

### 3.5 Periodization models [T]
- **Hierarchy:** macrocycle (often 1 year) -> mesocycles (weeks to months) -> microcycles (usually 1 week). GAS (Selye) is the theoretical basis: alarm -> resistance -> supercompensation, with exhaustion if overloaded.
- **Linear / traditional (Matveyev):** volume moves from high to low and intensity from low to high across phases.
  - **Preparatory:** hypertrophy/endurance (50-75% 1RM, 3-6 sets x 10-20), then basic strength (80-90%, 3-5 x 4-8).
  - **First transition:** strength/power (75-95%, 3-5 x 2-5).
  - **Competition:** peaking (>=93%, 1-3 x 1-3) or maintenance (80-85%, 2-3 x 3-6).
  - **Second transition (active rest):** 1-4 weeks of non-specific low-intensity work.
- **Undulating / nonlinear (DUP, WUP):** volume and intensity vary daily or weekly (e.g. Mon 3x12 hypertrophy, Wed 4x6 strength, Fri 5x3 power). Suits in-season athletes and general-population clients who need variety. Meta-analyses show similar or slightly better strength gains compared with linear models in trained subjects. The 2026 ACSM stand found periodization type does not consistently change outcomes.
- **Block (Issurin / Verkhoshansky):** concentrated 2-6 week blocks.
  - **Accumulation:** high volume, general abilities.
  - **Transmutation:** sport-specific, higher intensity.
  - **Realization:** taper and peak.
  - Uses residual training effects; suits advanced athletes with several competition peaks.
- **Tapering:** cut volume by about 40-60% while keeping intensity, over 1-2 weeks before competition.
- **Deload:** reduce volume and/or intensity roughly every 4th-6th week; this is common general-population practice.

### 3.6 1RM estimation formulas (use with <=10 reps; most accurate with 2-6 reps to failure) [T]
- **Epley (1985):** 1RM = w x (1 + reps/30) = w + w x reps x 0.0333
- **Brzycki (1993):** 1RM = w x 36 / (37 - reps) = w / (1.0278 - 0.0278 x reps)
- Lombardi: 1RM = w x reps^0.10
- O'Conner: 1RM = w x (1 + 0.025 x reps)
- Lander: 1RM = 100 x w / (101.3 - 2.67123 x reps)
- Example: 100 kg x 5 reps gives Epley 116.7 kg and Brzycki 112.5 kg. The two formulas agree exactly at 10 reps (133.3 kg).
- Inverse (working load for a target number of reps): w = 1RM x (37 - reps)/36 (Brzycki), or use the NSCA table.
- **Safety:** 1RM testing is not appropriate for novices, older adults, or anyone with uncontrolled hypertension, CVD or musculoskeletal issues. Use submaximal estimates (e.g. a 10RM or a 5RM) or RIR/RPE instead.
- **RIR-based RPE (Zourdos 2016):** RPE 10 = 0 RIR; 9 = 1 RIR; 8 = 2 RIR; 7 = 3 RIR; 5-6 = 4-6 RIR.

### 3.7 NSCA-CPT extras [T]
- Client consultation: informed consent, PAR-Q+, health history, lifestyle and goals.
- Standard tests:
  - **YMCA bench press:** 80 lb men / 35 lb women at 30 reps/min.
  - **Push-up test:** to failure.
  - **Partial curl-up.**
  - **Sit-and-reach.**
  - **YMCA 3-min step test:** 12-inch step, 96 bpm.
  - **Rockport 1-mile walk.**
  - **1.5-mile run.**
- NSCA warm-up model: **RAMP** (Raise, Activate/Mobilize, Potentiate). General warm-up 5-10 min, then a specific warm-up.
- Aerobic training: Karvonen and RPE; overtraining signs; progress no more than about 10% per week.

## 4. ACE — Integrated Fitness Training (ACE IFT) Model and behavior change

### 4.1 ACE IFT Model (current, ACE PT Manual 6th ed., July 2020 update) [V]
- **Foundation:** rapport, built through communication and trust. Behavior-change strategies run through every phase.
- **Two independent training components**, each with 3 phases. A client can be in different phases of each; progress in one does not "unlock" the other.

**Cardiorespiratory training (3-zone model anchored to ventilatory thresholds)**
- **Zone 1 (< VT1):** can talk comfortably; RPE about 3-4/10.
- **Zone 2 (VT1 to just below VT2):** talking is possible but not comfortable; RPE about 5-6.
- **Zone 3 (>= VT2):** talking is very difficult; RPE about 7-10.
- **VT1 assessment:** submaximal talk test (the HR at which reciting a passage first becomes uncomfortable).
- **VT2 assessment:** about a 15-20-min, 1-mile or 3-min test of the highest sustainable effort.
- ACE prefers measured HR at VT1/VT2 over %HRmax prediction equations.

| Phase | Focus | Zone distribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Base Training** | Build aerobic base; consistency; enjoyment; mostly steady-state | Mostly Zone 1. Clients cannot yet be assessed for VT2. |
| **Fitness Training** | Improve aerobic efficiency; add Zone 2 intervals; VT1 and VT2 assessed | Zone 1 and Zone 2, with some Zone 3 |
| **Performance Training** | Specific goals (racing, speed); anaerobic power | Polarized distribution, e.g. about 70-80% Zone 1, <10% Zone 2, 10-20% Zone 3 [!] (figures come from study aids) |

Older 4-phase version (pre-2020): aerobic base -> aerobic efficiency -> anaerobic endurance -> anaerobic power.

**Muscular training**

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Functional Training** | Postural stability and joint mobility. Core activation (proximal stability -> distal mobility), static balance, proper alignment. Good for deconditioned clients, those new to exercise, and those with postural deviations. |
| **Movement Training** | Train the **5 primary movement patterns** with good form at body weight or light load: **bend-and-lift (squat/hinge), single-leg, pushing, pulling, rotational**. |
| **Load/Speed Training** | Add external load for strength/hypertrophy (ACSM-style FITT), then speed/power (plyometrics, SAQ, Olympic-style lifts) for performance goals. The earlier model split this into "Load" and "Performance" phases. |

- **ACE Mover Method:** client-centered coaching using **ABC: Ask** (powerful open-ended questions), **Break down** barriers, **Collaborate** (co-create the plan).
- **Rapport stages** in the client relationship: rapport -> investigation -> planning -> action.
- Sources: [ACE "The New ACE IFT Model: What Has Changed" (2020)](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/july-2020/7580/the-new-ace-integrated-fitness-training-reg-model-what-has-changed/); [ACE threshold-based programming (Dalleck)](https://www.acefitness.org/education-and-resources/professional/certified/january-2021/7755/optimize-training-responsiveness-with-threshold-based-programming); [ACE IFT cardio phases (older)](https://www.acefitness.org/certifiednewsarticle/709/ace-ift-model-for-cardiorespiratory-training-phases-1-4/); [Practical application of the ACE IFT model](https://acefitness.org/blog/5002/practical-application-of-the-ace-ift-model); [open-exam-prep ACE 1.3](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ace-cpt/introduction/ace-ift-and-mover-method).

### 4.2 Transtheoretical Model (Stages of Change; Prochaska & DiClemente) [T]

| Stage | Definition | Coaching strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Precontemplation | Not intending to change in the next 6 months | Raise awareness, give information, explore pros of change, avoid pressure |
| Contemplation | Intends to change within 6 months; ambivalent | Decisional balance (pros vs cons), motivational interviewing, address barriers, build self-efficacy |
| Preparation | Intends to act within 30 days; some activity, but below guidelines | Concrete plan, SMART goals, social support, schedule sessions |
| Action | Meets activity criteria for **<6 months** (highest relapse risk) | Self-monitoring, reinforcement, relapse-prevention planning, stimulus control |
| Maintenance | Has met the criteria for **>=6 months** | Variety, new goals, preventing boredom and burnout, coping with high-risk situations |

- Other TTM constructs:
  - **Processes of change.** Cognitive ones dominate early stages: consciousness raising, dramatic relief, environmental reevaluation, self-reevaluation, social liberation. Behavioral ones dominate later stages: self-liberation, counterconditioning, stimulus control, reinforcement management, helping relationships.
  - **Decisional balance.**
  - **Self-efficacy.**
- Relapse is normal; recycling through stages is expected.

### 4.3 Motivational interviewing (Miller & Rollnick) [T]
- **Spirit (PACE):** Partnership, Acceptance, Compassion, Evocation. The client is the expert on their own life.
- **Core skills (OARS):** **O**pen-ended questions, **A**ffirmations, **R**eflective listening (simple and complex reflections), **S**ummarizing.
- **Processes:** engaging -> focusing -> evoking -> planning.
- **Change talk (DARN-CAT):**
  - Preparatory: Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need.
  - Mobilizing: Commitment, Activation, Taking steps.
  - Elicit and reinforce it, and soften sustain talk.
- **Rolling with resistance / discord:** avoid the "righting reflex", do not argue, reflect back, emphasize autonomy.
- **Scaling questions:** "On a scale of 0-10, how important is it... why not a lower number?" and "What would it take to move up one point?"
- **Elicit-Provide-Elicit (Ask-Offer-Ask)** for giving information.

### 4.4 Goal setting and self-efficacy [T]
- **SMART goals:** Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Time-bound. Example: "Walk 30 min at RPE 12-13 on Mon/Wed/Fri before work for the next 4 weeks."
- Balance **process goals** (behaviors the client controls) with **outcome goals** (results), and short-term with long-term goals. Review every 2-4 weeks.
- **Self-efficacy** (Bandura) is the strongest predictor of adherence. Its sources:
  1. Past performance / mastery experiences (strongest)
  2. Vicarious experience
  3. Verbal persuasion
  4. Physiological and emotional states
  5. Imaginal experiences
- Other models: Health Belief Model; Self-Determination Theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness); Theory of Planned Behavior; locus of control; operant conditioning (antecedents, consequences, shaping).
- Adherence factors:
  - Personal: self-efficacy, prior experience.
  - Environmental: time, access, social support.
  - Program: intensity (high intensity lowers adherence), convenience, group vs individual.
- About **50% of people who start an exercise program drop out within 6 months** (classic ACE/ACSM figure).

Sources: [ACE health coach / behavior change article](https://www.acefitness.org/education-and-resources/professional/expert-articles/5400/the-role-of-a-health-coach-and-behavior-change-specialist/).

## 5. PAR-Q+ and red flags; scope of practice

### 5.1 PAR-Q+ (PAR-Q+ Collaboration; 2025 version current, a 2026 form also circulates) [V for structure and Q1; T for the remaining wording]
**Page 1 — 7 general health questions (YES/NO):**
1. Has your doctor ever said that you have a heart condition **OR** high blood pressure?
2. Do you feel pain in your chest at rest, during your daily activities of living, **OR** when you do physical activity?
3. Do you lose balance because of dizziness **OR** have you lost consciousness in the last 12 months? (Answer NO if your dizziness was associated with over-breathing, including during vigorous exercise.)
4. Have you ever been diagnosed with another chronic medical condition (other than heart disease or high blood pressure)?
5. Are you currently taking prescribed medications for a chronic medical condition?
6. Do you currently have (or have you had within the past 12 months) a bone, joint, or soft tissue (muscle, ligament, or tendon) problem that could be made worse by becoming more physically active? (Answer NO if you had a problem in the past but it does not limit your current ability to be physically active.)
7. Has your doctor ever said that you should only do medically supervised physical activity?

**If all answers are NO:** the client is cleared for physical activity. Start slowly and build up gradually, and follow international physical activity guidelines for their age. People over 45 who are not used to regular vigorous-to-maximal effort should consult a qualified exercise professional before such exercise. A fitness assessment is optional.

**If any answer is YES:** complete pages 2-3 (follow-up questions). Condition categories:
1. arthritis / osteoporosis / back problems
2. cancer
3. heart or cardiovascular condition
4. high blood pressure
5. metabolic conditions (diabetes)
6. mental health / learning disability
7. respiratory disease
8. spinal cord injury
9. stroke
10. other / multiple conditions

Typical sub-question themes: whether the condition is controlled with medication or other therapies; recent hypoglycemia or other complications; resting BP >=160/90; whether the client has fainted. If every follow-up answer is NO, the client is cleared. **Any YES on a follow-up means referral to a qualified exercise professional and/or completing ePARmed-X+ online / seeing a physician.**

**Delay becoming more active if:**
- the client has a temporary illness such as a cold or fever (wait until better)
- the client is **pregnant**: talk to a health care practitioner and/or complete the ePARmed-X+ (or Get Active Questionnaire for Pregnancy)
- the client's health changes: answer the PAR-Q+ again, and if a new YES appears, consult a professional

The PAR-Q+ is valid for 12 months or until health changes.

Sources: [PAR-Q+ 2025 form PDF (hosted)](https://southlake.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/PARQPlus2025-Form-fillable.pdf); [2025 PAR-Q+ form (jotform)](https://form.jotform.com/221436864485060); official site: eparmedx.com.

### 5.2 Red flags requiring medical referral (consolidated for an AI coach)
**STOP and refer (urgent if acute):**
- Chest pain, pressure, tightness or discomfort (chest, jaw, neck, arm, back), at rest or with exertion. **Possible emergency: call emergency services.**
- Fainting or near-fainting, or dizziness/light-headedness during or after exercise.
- Unusual shortness of breath at rest or with mild exertion; orthopnea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea.
- Palpitations, racing or irregular heartbeat.
- Calf pain with walking that resolves with rest (claudication), or unilateral calf swelling, pain or redness (possible DVT).
- Ankle swelling (edema); known heart murmur.
- Unusual fatigue with usual activities.
- Resting BP >=200/110 (or >=160/90 per PAR-Q+ follow-up); during exercise, SBP >250 or DBP >115, or SBP falling as workload rises.
- Diabetes: BG >250 mg/dL with ketones; repeated hypoglycemia; new numbness or foot wounds; vision changes.
- Neurological: sudden severe headache, slurred speech, facial droop, one-sided weakness (stroke: emergency).
- Musculoskeletal: sharp, worsening or radiating pain; numbness or tingling; joint instability or locking; swelling after injury; pain that changes gait; night pain; pain lasting more than a few days after training; suspected fracture or acute injury.
- Pregnancy warning signs (see section 2.5).
- Unexplained weight loss, fever, or symptoms of an eating disorder or RED-S (missed periods, restrictive eating); expressions of self-harm (refer to mental-health or crisis resources).
- Recent surgery, hospitalization, new diagnosis or new medication.
- Answering YES on the PAR-Q+, or showing ACSM signs/symptoms.

### 5.3 Scope of practice for personal trainers (ACE, NASM, ACSM, NSCA consensus) [V]

**Trainers DO:**
- Screen (PAR-Q+, health history); obtain informed consent and medical clearance when indicated.
- Run fitness and movement assessments, which are not diagnostic.
- Design and progress exercise programs for apparently healthy clients, or for clients with medical clearance and guidance.
- Teach general, evidence-based nutrition and healthy eating that aligns with public guidelines (Dietary Guidelines for Americans, MyPlate, hydration, general protein guidance).
- Coach behavior change; support goals; motivate.
- Recognize red flags and **refer** (physician, physical therapist, registered dietitian, mental-health professional). Respond to emergencies (CPR/AED certification is required).
- Document and keep client information confidential (HIPAA-like practice).

**Trainers DO NOT:**
- **Diagnose** any medical condition or injury ("it sounds like a torn meniscus" is out of scope; "that warrants evaluation by a physician or PT" is in scope).
- **Treat, rehabilitate or "fix" injuries**, pain or disease. Post-rehab work happens only after discharge from, and in collaboration with, the PT or physician.
- **Prescribe** medication, or advise starting, stopping or changing medication or insulin doses.
- **Prescribe diets or individualized meal plans**, especially for medical conditions (diabetes, kidney disease, eating disorders, food allergies, pregnancy complications). That is medical nutrition therapy, the scope of an RD/RDN. ACE: "Don't ... provide clients with specific meal plans" unless credentialed.
- **Recommend or sell supplements** as treatment, or make health claims.
- Provide psychological counseling or therapy.
- Massage or manipulate joints (unless separately licensed); give medical advice; interpret ECGs or lab values beyond general education.
- Exceed their competence. State laws vary; some US states license nutrition advice.

Sources: [ACE nutrition scope of practice (Nitschke)](https://www.acefitness.org/education-and-resources/professional/expert-articles/6248/nutrition-scope-of-practice-what-you-can-do-as-a-personal-trainer); [ACE health coach role (Muth)](https://www.acefitness.org/education-and-resources/professional/expert-articles/5400/the-role-of-a-health-coach-and-behavior-change-specialist/); [NFPT meal plan or not](https://nfpt.com/meal-plan-not-meal-plan/); [open-exam-prep ACE scope](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/ace-cpt/ethics-business/scope-ethics-professional-conduct); [open-exam-prep ACSM scope](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/acsm-cpt/chapter-1-foundations/acsm-scope-of-practice).

## 6. Synthesis for the AI coaching skill (decision rules)

1. **Intake gate:**
   - Run the PAR-Q+ (7 Qs) and ask about current exercise habits (ACSM: >=30 min moderate, 3 d/wk for 3 months).
   - Any symptom from the ACSM signs/symptoms list means **no programming**: refer for medical clearance.
   - Known disease with no symptoms: light-moderate only, and recommend clearance.
2. **Assess:**
   - Movement screen (OHSA-style self-check: feet, knees, lumbar, arms) -> map findings to the overactive/underactive table -> add inhibit/lengthen/activate/integrate to the warm-up.
   - Include a disclaimer that this is not a diagnosis.
3. **Choose the phase** (NASM / ACE equivalence):
   - Beginner or deconditioned: Stabilization / Functional + Base (12-20 reps, 50-70%, slow tempo, Zone 1).
   - Then Strength-endurance / Movement + Fitness.
   - Then Hypertrophy (6-12, 67-85%, about 10+ sets/muscle/wk), Max Strength (1-5 at >=85%, 3-5 min rest), Power (30-70% moved fast, or a contrast pair).
4. **Dose by goal** (NSCA table) and **estimate loads** from reps using Epley/Brzycki or the %1RM table. Prefer RIR 1-3 over true 1RM tests for general clients.
5. **Cardio by FITT-VP:** 150-300 min moderate or 75-150 vigorous; intensity by %HRR (Karvonen), RPE or talk test (VT1/VT2 zones).
6. **Periodize:**
   - Linear for novices.
   - Undulating for variety, busy schedules or plateaus.
   - Block for advanced clients with event dates.
   - Deload every 4-6 weeks.
   - Remember (ACSM 2026) that adherence beats the choice of model.
7. **Special populations:** apply the modified FITT and safety rules (BP thresholds, glucose thresholds, ACOG pregnancy rules, older-adult balance and power work).
8. **Coach behavior:** identify the TTM stage, use OARS/MI and change talk, set SMART process goals, and build self-efficacy through early mastery.
9. **Scope guardrails:** never diagnose, treat injuries, adjust meds, or write medical/therapeutic meal plans. Give general nutrition guidance and refer to an MD/PT/RD/mental-health professional when needed.
